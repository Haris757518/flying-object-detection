Detection of flying object using Hybrid deep learning Techniques Karan Chander S R, Haris Kkaran.chandersr2024@vitstudent.ac.in, haris.k2024@vitstudent.ac.inI. Introduction: Aerial objects such as airplanes, helicopters, drones, birds etc are increasing and are posing complexity in the management of airspace. An intelligent system that detects and discriminates between these targets is essential to address the security, observation, and military constraints. Aerial targets are expected to appear in the frames at the same time from surveillance environments, airports, forbidden areas, military regions and urban territories. Robust detection and classification can aid Air-Traffic Management, Collision Avoidance, border surveillance and Security, Public Safety, wildlife protection, unauthorized drone detection, etc.Detection of aerial objects is a difficult task in computer vision, considering factors such as: objects that are small, distant, partially occluded or captured against clutter backgrounds. Different flying object classes at varying scales, aspect angles and flight altitudes are also challenging due to their variance and appearance changes. For instance, a far seen drone would seem like a bird and a distant helicopter, and airplane would get mistaken for the other just by based on the few limited visual cues. Factors like continuously moving camera, cluttered backgrounds, low lighting, haze, rain etc are significant issues which may lead to reduced reliability of a conventional detector.The conventional approach is built on the concept of feature descriptors such as Edges, Contours, Color, Texture and Shape. However, their accuracy and robustness under complex environments and less common presentations can be reduced in scenarios like: unusual orientations, overlapping silhouettes of object and many variations in the appearance of objects, low-resolution. Additionally, the spatial and temporal relationships between objects in the scene cannot directly be utilized.With advances in deep learning techniques, object detectors learning features directly from the data have been successful in recognizing aerial objects from the data with high performance. CNN models for detecting objects automatically learn the high-level features that constitute them (e.g. For aerial vehicles, spatial features such as, edges, textures, shapes and structural characteristics of an aerial object. The modern detection framework such as the one-stage and two-stage detectors use these features to localize the detected object while, classifying it to one of the target classes that it was trained for.When the problem scope comes down to identifying each frame individually, a frame-based object detector often does not utilize its motion profile, which can be quite valuable as different kinds of flying objects exhibit varied behaviour when in motion. Hybrid deep learning models, however, combine a CNN, to find the spatial characteristics, with an LSTM/GRU/Transformer architecture to learn the temporal correlations. Attention models may also be used to refine the objects with larger attention weight on its dominant parts and feature streams. An auxiliary object tracking component may further enhance the system by predicting future positions of the object and therefore identifying it. Hence combining these varied sources of information about objects flying can help us achieve very good results.Here, the proposed research work of "Flying Object Detection Using Hybrid Deep Learning Techniques" is envisioned to build an intelligent system that can distinguish, locate and categorize an assortment of aerial objects - including airplanes, helicopter, drones, birds and others under diverse conditions of their occurrences and characteristics. The system will be designed to take on general category recognition (multi-class object detection) as opposed to just targeting the discrimination of drones or a drone and bird classification. The ultimate outcome is expected to present each distinct aerial object's presence in the frame as well as its class.The proposed architecture would incorporate an object detector based on the CNN architecture in parallel with a module that utilizes time/ motion characteristics. It consists of a CNN model used for acquiring spatial visual features from each frame and another module which leverages time, motion aspects or attention over various feature levels and channels while also possibly incorporating a track predictor to detect instances. It should alleviate the detection challenge of small objects, low resolutions images, occluded targets in clutter backgrounds or against rapidly changing environments, while localizing the target, assigning appropriate class in a unified detection scheme.System will be designed to provide object location and category-based detection results. The system will be rigorously evaluated for different measures like, precision, recall, F1-score, mean average precision (mAP), IoU, and confusion matrices along with the processing speed of the system. Performance will be evaluated for individual class instances along with generalized performance on all targets. Accuracy based measure along with the processing rate will play a crucial role as this system can be utilized to process the real time surveillance videos.II. Literature ReviewH. Lee et al. [1] developed a decision-level sensor-fusion method for UAV detection using CNNs in an approach where three separate CNN paths were trained on optical camera images, radar range-Doppler maps, and audio spectrograms, respectively, and then merged using a multinomial logistic regression step. With multi-sensor data gathered from the field (rather than simulation alone), the combined system produced detection accuracies up to 15.6% higher than any one sensor CNN approach, illustrating the importance of heterogeneous sensing across sensors for robust airspace safety.M. U. Khan et al. [2] introduced SafeSpace MFNet - a modification of YOLOv5s by incorporating feature-attention module to the multi-feature network, which was trained on small, medium, and large versions over a custom and open-sourced large dataset of birds and UAVs under various scale of objects and environmental settings. The largest variant obtained 98.4% mean average precision, 96.6% recall, and 98.3% mAP, and the second largest variant reported 99.8% precision of bird detection to help set up the first reliable and open-source reproducible benchmark against bird vs drone (not differentiating between UAV types). However, one concern is that SafeSpace MFNet is set for binary classification (birds versus drone), but not specifically type of drone.X. Zhang et al. [3] presented an hybrid Tracking-Classification approach, which combined an IMM filter with an LSTM-based Recurrent Neural Network trained using kinematics flight-trajectory data of small UAS and birds tracked with radar. The classifier operated on flight-manoeuvre characterization in contrast of their appearance which resulted in effective performances over various weather and distances for which vision-based methods do not succeed, and proved beneficial in support of systems, operating in degraded conditions (low visibility).M. Alshaer et al. [4] fused the output from a deep learning-based visual detector and a Kalman filter to track objects over time and generated their own data from the real world in order to classify UAVs from birds and air-vehicles in poor weather and complicated scenery. The detection–tracking–classification hybrid system was successful in the unfavourable conditions and solved the lack of UAV-type detection in recent papers which, according to them, has not been addressed well.M. Ke et al. [5] Proposed a targeted low-altitude small-target trajectory dataset LAT-BirdDrone, recorded using a three-band photoelectric turntable and extracted with YOLOv11n and ByteTrack. They carried out the benchmark on the transformer +lstm +cnn composite hybrid model, independent cnn, transformer, lstm and cnn+Bilstm models, and they verified that the hybrid composite model outperforms all other models for this classification task and encourages more comparable trajectory-based benchmark results for micro-target bird-vs-drone discrimination.A multimodal data fusion recognition network introduced in study in Complex & Intelligent Systems by Zhu et al. [6] to integrate visual appearance as well as behavioural/ motion evidence that was applied in long-range contexts to discriminate between an unmanned aerial vehicle and bird, where a traditional shape-based perception approach would be indecisive. According to researchers, their multimodal fusion method showed superiority over single-modality basis such as millimetre-wave-radar based single-modality classification and other cross-modal semantic alignment methods, which contributes both airspace-safety as well as ecological-protection purposes.Fu et al. [7], proposed FDGAF-CNN. This model transforms the 1-dimensional RF time series signals into 2-dimensional time-frequency pictures of Gramian Angular Field images which were sent to the CNN. We implemented and evaluated on the published dataset DroneRF and DroneRFa, the accuracy can reach up to 98.72% and 98.67% on DroneRF and DroneRFa respectively; and some class could reach to 100%; however, since these signals are too similar, there are still some confusion between drone models that belong to spectrally adjacent models.Zhang et al. [8] proposed a lightweight CNN using a coordinate-attention mechanism to realize classification targets of the Range-Doppler Map obtained by FMCW radar for drones, vehicle and pedestrians. The network could achieve a higher accuracy with much less computational cost, the work is very well-suited to real-time embedded radar system and enhanced its abilities to discriminate feature in RDM compared with the normal CNN design.Ni, J., & Zhou, Z. [9] proposed UAVsNet, which is combined with self-gated hybrid attention and large-kernel attention module to the standard CNN backbone with pruning strategy of Taylor series expansion, was also presented on STFT time-frequency images of FMCW radar signal. The performance of pruned attention network-based model exceeds standard CNN and previous multiscale decision-fusion CNN but has fewer parameters and FLOPs to deploy on the small UAV to recognize micro-Doppler.N. Yu et al. Proposed SMNet [10], a deep spectrogram-based network designed to perform flight-control RF signal detection and classify multiple simultaneously flown drone signals. Rather than exploring a restricted single-drone scenario often considered inRF classification approaches, SMNet dealt with the more challenging multi-drone problem formulation-appropriate for a swarm or multi-intrusion detection scenario.Z. Ghouli and Zakaria [11] integrated Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA) estimation and a Random Forest classifier in a distributed MEMS microphone array setup to enable low-cost, passive acoustic detection and localization of drones. Experiments carried out in real-field conditions revealed the robustness of the system to environmental variations and a feasible configuration in low-cost, visually obscured deployments, whereas the non-deep nature of the classifier limited the capacity of representation-learning of its state-of-the-art CNN/RNN counterparts.Lakshmi et al.[12] compared head to head YOLOv5s against YOLOv8 and introduced a new three-class Avian-Airborne detection benchmark that included aircraft, birds and UAVs, against the now well used Det-Fly benchmark we report YOLOv8 reaches 99.5% mAP on Det-Fly while the new detection benchmark gives results on that dataset with an mAP of 99.6%; the fact of providing a true three class public benchmark goes a long way to fixing the lack of benchmarks past binary drone vs background classifications.Megha Kataria, Brejesh Lall [13] used YOLOv5 detection with centroid-based object tracking to obtain a set of object trajectories, which was fed into an LSTM network for the recognition of motion patterns. An additional ResNet-18 network branch was used for identifying the distortion types on the thermal imagery. By successfully achieving an mAP50:95 of 0.615, a precision of 0.995, and a recall of 0.973, YOLOv5 as a perception model, and using combined motion patterns and appearance-based detection achieved improved robustness against low illumination on thermal images.M. A. Khan et al. [14] provide an extensive overview of unauthorized-drone-detection methods across various sensing techniques - radar, RF, acoustic and vision - summarizing the training environments and achieved results in tens of previous papers. These authors state that, Although there exist quite many single-modality approaches to detect unauthorized drones, fair comparison of performances and complete sensor fusion solutions for unauthorized-drone detection approaches are still immature, thus encouraging to adopt standard sensor fusion frameworks in the near future.M. Z. Anwar et al. [15] published an early work that employed an ML based acoustic classifier to detect amateur drones. Sound features from specially captured drone audio were collected against common background noises of significance for public safety. The detection performance is not quantified, however, this work highlights the potential of utilizing a inexpensive sensor like an acoustics-based system for public-safety-related objectives.Ajith, V. S., & Jolly, K. G. [16] developed an enhanced Region-based CNN incorporating a mix ofhand-crafted ResNet,SIFTandLocal Gabor Binary Pattern (LGBP) features, weight-balanced via a metaheuristic weighting scheme influenced byHoney-Badger-Algorithmfor the detection of objects captured from the view ofa drone that was performing search-and-rescue missions. A deep-plus-hand-craftedfeature combined pipeline can be a viable option given there is limited training dataset though the cameraviewpoint, angle and height was the challenge.Aote et al. (2024) [17]  created a benchmark of a CNN-LSTM approach in comparison to a CNN-based examination of a YOLO-family of objects, by joining spatial aspects that originate from a CNN to time-dependence aspects modelled from an LSTM, of videoframes for far away small point sized distant flying entities derived from sources like the GitHub, the UCI, and at ICCV affiliated places. A similar spatiotemporality applied to address that a single frame may produce low-confidence predictions of far-off objects with small surfaceG. Ganganath et al. [18] applied a transformer-encoder based model to the fusion of FMCW radar raw signals, that is, directly using the input data instead of converting the signal to a spectrogram or Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients(MFCCs), with directional microphone acoustic signals for jointly detecting and classifying drones, and tested on its own data set (released in the public domain outdoors) which also exceeded traditional methods in performance by increasing model speed and reducing the number of parameters.A. Coluccia et al. [19] facilitated the “Drone-vs-Bird Detection” grand challenge, and critically judged three autonomously constructed, in-the-wild video inspection detection pipelines(ranging from CNN-based frame-based detection and CNN-U Net segmentation to spatiotemporal-segmentation approaches). In no instance did 1 method outperform all other methods and the difficult case for all the algorithms were those taken with distant camera view and movements, so it created a public available standard dataset with standard inspection metrics that is also being referred from now by researchers regarding drone-vs-bird surveillance tasks.S. K. Shandilya et al. [20] developed their “Drone-vs-Bird” dataset collection that was labelled using Roboflow to produce YOLOv7 PyTorch format images with per-pixel object mask rather than per-object bounding box annotations. This type of instance-level segmentation annotation was beneficial as it provides contour cues useful for avoiding common classification confusions between drone and birds.J. Leng et al. [21] introduced an aerial object detection literature review and categorized the dispersed findings in the fields into five challenge types of scale, space, object, semantics, and class due to contrast performance on VisDrone-DET and DOTA benchmark datasets, suggesting that few detectors deal with one or two imbalanced types collectively and united handling of multiple issues remains relatively less investigated.W. Hua and Q. Chen [22] reported a systematic review of deep-learning methods that were dedicated to detecting small targets in aerial and remote-sensing images, with categories including feature extraction and promotion, feature fusion in multiple scales, and attention mechanism methods. The review noted that aerial scene detection was widely reported but those works targeting on specifically optimizing very small scale, low resolution object detection are rarely found; on the contrary, weight-based detectors could be designed to be deployed on the UAV.Al-Zadjali et al. [23] developed STBRNN: A Spatio-temporal Bio-Response Neural Network which leveraged a Bio-Inspired CNN for spatial features, Gated Recurrent Units for temporal movement dynamics, and a new Bio-Response Layer that modifies attention (on spatial and temporal levels) according to the intensity, proximity, and velocity consistency of an object’s motion. When compared to existing detectors (YOLOv5, Faster R-CNN, SSD, RetinaNet, and R-FCN),STBRNN yielded optimal results (precision: 0.984; recall: 0.964; F1-score: 0.974; IoU: 0.96; inference speed: 45 ms), focusing explicitly on the difference in motion between UAVs andbirds as ‘erratic’ vs ‘rhythmic,’while being efficient for near-realtime applications.For small flying-bird detection in surveillance video, Z.-W. Sun et al. [24] built a flight-bird detection dataset in surveillance video (namely FBD-SV-2024), including 483 real videos and more than 28,000 bird objects with annotations, and evaluated some baseline object detection frameworks (an Co-Attention-FA and an attendantFBOD-Net). It is shown that even the modern video object detectors still have much work to do due to the small objects, diverse shapes, and the natural environment in this dataset.N. Habash et al. Conducted a survey [25] of real-time aerial object detection approaches emphasizing the constraints of the onboard and edge environment. This review analyzed lightweight backbones, multi-scale fusion, and hardware-aware techniques of quantization, pruning, and TensorRT for acceleration. In summary of the surveyed literature on RGB-based edge-computing detection, 14 to 47 FPS on NVIDIA Jetson series devices was reported, whereas a lack of unified optimization on accuracy, latency, and power Consumption for true onboard UAV scenario was perceived.S. Luesutthiviboon et al. [26] developed a classification network using trajectory (motion-over-time) features alongside visual appearance features; trained and evaluated on a custom dataset of infrared drone-and-bird image sequences encompassing diverse drone appearances and flight strategies. Their trajectory-augmented classification outperformed frame-wise appearance-only classification by as much as 22%, with greater gains on small, fast objects, demonstrating how trajectory can contribute as a valuable additional classification signal.Together, these papers offer a convincing view of a clear, upward progress in the UAV-versus-object classification area: from the use of primitive single-modality acoustic and RF features in basic classification architectures [15] to a multitude of vision-based classification detector modules [2], [12], [19], [20]; from appearance-only models to hybrid approaches incorporating trajectory and motion information, improving significantly for tiny/distant/fast moving objects [3], [5], [13], [23], [26]; and from the use of single-sensor information to fusion of multi-modal (radar/audio/RF/vision) information, either at feature level or decision level [1], [6], [18]. Across survey and overview papers [14], [21], [22], [25], a unanimous sentiment is reached: even though individual components of UAV detection have evolved considerably, joint, multi-class benchmarks, feature level fusion pipelines and accuracy-latency-power co-optimization hardware designs for actual flight deployment need to be developed to make this field much more mature.S.No.Title & YearDataset UsedAlgorithm UsedPerformance MeasureFuture Work1CNN-Based UAV Detection and Classification Using Sensor Fusion (2023)Field-collected drone dataset (optical images, radar range-Doppler maps, audio spectrograms) + open drone databasesThree-branch CNN (optical, radar-RDM,audio-spectrogram) fused via multinomial logistic regressionSensor fusion improves accuracy by up to 15.6% over any single-sensor CNN baselineExploring feature-level (early/intermediate) fusion architectures instead of only decision-level fusion2SafeSpace MFNet: Precise and Efficient MultiFeature Drone Detection Network (2023)Custom open-sourced bird-vs-UAV dataset (multiple environments, object sizes, drone dimensions)MFNet / MFNet-FA: YOLOv5s-based multi-scalemulti-feature CNN with feature-attention module (S/M/L variants)MFNet-L: 98.4% AP, 96.6% recall, 98.3% mAP,72.8% IoU;
